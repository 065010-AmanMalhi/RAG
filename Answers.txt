Urban residents in India face specific structural risk factors beyond income: social isolation driven by nuclear family structures, the breakdown of community support systems, competitive employment markets, housing insecurity, and the adjustment challenges faced by rural-to-urban migrants. Urban areas also show higher rates of substance use disorders (approximately 20.4% of urban males have alcohol use disorders per NMHS data). Common mental disorders affect 10.6% of urban adults vs 6.9% of rural adults.

The initiative described is TELE-MANAS (Telemental Health Assistance and Networking Across States), launched in 2022. It was designed to be free, multilingual, and accessible via a basic phone call — addressing the exclusion created by app-based platforms that require smartphones, English proficiency, and internet access. The limitation of digital solutions broadly is that they largely serve English-proficient, smartphone-owning urban residents and do little for the urban poor, who lack digital literacy or connectivity. Even TELE-MANAS's effectiveness depends on implementation quality, public awareness, and integration with other health services.
The Mental Healthcare Act 2017 decriminalised attempted suicide by amending Section 309 of the Indian Penal Code. Beyond this, the Act: (1) established the right to mental healthcare as a legally enforceable right, (2) prohibited forced treatment except in specified circumstances, and (3) introduced advance directives allowing individuals with mental illness to specify their treatment preferences in advance. However, implementation has been slow — state-level mental health authorities required by the Act remain non-functional in several states.
